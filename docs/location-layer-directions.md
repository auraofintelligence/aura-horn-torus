# Places for Joyful Responsible Abundance

Suggested directions, 5 September 2026. These categories and source leads have **not been imported**. They are proposals for review, not claims that listed organisations are affiliated with, endorse or have been assessed for alignment with GAJRA Earth or Aura of Intelligence.

The useful next step is to connect places through what people can do together: learn, make, repair, grow, observe, restore, share and celebrate. Cities provide the setting; universities can provide knowledge and research pathways; these additional layers would reveal the everyday places where participation becomes possible.

This interpretation starts with the user's [GAJRA Earth association invitation](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/gajra-earth-claude-build/) and its open questions, with the [related public-practice project](https://auraofintelligence.github.io/GAJRA-earth-infinity/) as supporting context. Joy includes play, belonging and curiosity; responsibility includes consent, care and ecological limits; abundance includes time, knowledge, access and shared capability. None of those values can be established from a business category alone.

## Make the categories work together

A useful first view would be **“What can we do around this place?”** Selecting a city or university could reveal nearby opportunities to learn, borrow, grow, help and enjoy. Distance would indicate proximity only; an actual organisational relationship would require a source of its own.

Three example journeys to test with real records:

- **Learn → make → repair → share:** a library workshop, makerspace, repair session and tool library.
- **Observe → restore → grow → celebrate:** a citizen science activity, habitat-care group, community garden and public gathering space.
- **Plan → provide → participate:** a community venue, a documented community-energy project, and nearby drinking water and suitable toilets.

These are possible combinations, not existing partnerships. To make them synchronous in time, attach dated sessions to their venue and preserve the organiser's timezone, booking link and cancellation source. A monthly repair event should not appear to be open every day.

## A manageable first collection

Start with a small South East Queensland collection across **repair/sharing, food growing, learning/making, habitat care and gathering**, then apply the same tests to Aotearoa New Zealand and the wider Oceania collection. Add practical amenities around those places. Global directories can extend that pattern once each source's coverage and reuse terms are understood.

For each candidate keep: source link and source identifier; checked date; current status; venue versus service-area geometry; coordinate source and accuracy; what a visitor can do; published costs, access and booking conditions; and reuse/attribution requirements. Preserve unknowns explicitly. A shared venue can have several activities without becoming several duplicate place pins.

First Nations knowledge and stewardship should be represented through information the relevant community or organisation has deliberately made public. Public visitor information does not authorise publishing sensitive cultural places or ecological observation coordinates. Personal reflections, saved journeys and inferred preferences remain private unless someone deliberately shares them.

The review question is practical: **Can a person use this record to find a real opportunity, understand its conditions, and choose whether to take part?** Category membership alone should never become an automated score of virtue, trust, spiritual resonance or affiliation.
